In plain words

What LIBERVERSE is, and what it is not.

What is it?

A set of online lessons about the digital world: keeping information private, reading data, writing code, using AI carefully, designing a city and making fair rules. It is one of three ARK Learning Lab pathways.

Who is it for?

Learners aged 6 to 18, with a parent, guardian or pod facilitator. The learner picks one age group and works through its six modules, then a four-week capstone project.

Where does it happen?

Online, on a computer or tablet, with a print version of every module for screen-free days. Pods can meet at their own venue. Mentor meetings are online. Nothing happens at ARK Village in Serbia yet.

What does the learner end up with?

A private portfolio: one piece of work from each module, plus a capstone project shown to a mentor. The Proof Kit prototype shows how that work could later be checked without sharing the learner’s name.

Is it a school?

No. It is a draft enrichment programme, not an accredited school or qualification. Families stay responsible for local education rules.

Can we join today?

Not yet. Enrolment and payments are not open. You can read every module, use the materials library and try the free tools now.

How it works

Four steps, start to finish.

  1. Pick the learner's age group.Each of the four groups has its own six modules.
  2. Do one module at a time.Each module is 6 lessons. One lesson a week is a good pace. Any order.
  3. Keep the work.After each module the learner files one thing they made in the private portfolio.
  4. Finish with a capstone.After all six modules: a 4-week project that pulls it all together, shown to a mentor.
Where LIBERVERSE happens

Mostly online at home, on a computer or tablet. Every module also has a print version for screen-free days. Pods can run it at their own venue. Mentor meetings are online. ARK Village in Apatin, Serbia, is not open yet: no lessons or visit days run there now.

One year is about 40 weeks.

6 modules × 6 lessons = 36 lessons, plus a 4-week capstone. Go slower if you need to. Nothing is timed.

One lesson

90 minutes, always in the same order.

  1. 15 min · LearnA short video, reading or audio explains the idea.
  2. 30 min · TryThe learner copies a worked example, step by step.
  3. 30 min · BuildThey work on their own project choice.
  4. 15 min · ShowThey file the work and write one sentence about it.

The parent/guardian's job: set up the time and space, check the work is filed, and ask “what did you make today?” You don't need to know how to code. The mentor reviews the work online.

Safety rules

What we never do.

  • No crypto wallets, tokens or payments for learners. Ever.
  • No learner records on any blockchain. The Proof Kit prototype can add only an anonymous fingerprint to a test network, and only if a parent opts in: never a name, file, score or location.
  • Cameras are optional. Paper and audio work count.
  • All votes and ledgers are practice games, not official Liberland processes.
  • Learners under 13 use accounts set up and managed by a parent/guardian.
  • Every module has a print-friendly, low-internet version.
