Learning design · ages 6–18

The 7 Continuums of Learner Agency

A way to think about how a learner’s voice, choices and ownership can grow over time. At ARK Learning Lab, self-direction is coached: clear teaching and adult support remain while learners take on more decisions.

Reflection framework · not a diagnostic or validated assessment

Structured self-direction

Stage 2 is the bridge, not the deep end.

Learners do not need to arrive already independent. Adults teach essential knowledge, help agree a meaningful goal and make success criteria visible. Choices and responsibility expand as the learner practises and shows readiness.

Stage 1 · Guided foundation

Learn with clear structure

Adults introduce core ideas, model the task and set a clear purpose. Learners practise routines and make manageable choices with support.

Stage 2 · Co-designed bridge

Plan the work together

Learner and adult agree the question, timeline, evidence and checkpoints. The mentor teaches or adds scaffolds when the work shows a gap.

Stage 3 · Learner-led work

Take meaningful ownership

The learner proposes a route, builds and revises the work, explains decisions and reflects on responsibility. Adults remain available and accountable.

Not a one-way ladder.

A learner can lead in one area and want more guidance in another. Needs change with the task, subject, experience and circumstances. The goal is appropriate support and real learning, not a stage label.

What families can expect

Choice with checkpoints.

Core knowledge is taught

Projects do not replace explicit instruction, guided practice or checks for understanding.

Goals and evidence are agreed

A learner’s question is connected to shared outcomes. The work and what counts as evidence are made clear before a project begins.

Adults keep responsibility

Teachers, mentors and guardians retain responsibility for teaching, supervision, safety, access and lawful operation.

Seven dimensions · different ways agency can show up

Not one score. Seven useful conversations.

The framework looks at separate dimensions of learning. A learner’s pattern may be mixed. Use these descriptions to discuss support and next steps, not to rank learners.

01 · Voice

From expression to leadership

Can the learner share ideas, influence how learning happens and communicate a perspective to others?

02 · Choice

From options to autonomy

Does the learner select from clear options, customize a route or propose a project of their own?

03 · Engagement

From participation to inquiry

Is the learner completing a guided task, investing in a co-designed goal or pursuing a sustained question?

04 · Motivation

From external prompts to purpose

What is helping the learner persist: structure, curiosity, growing mastery or a purpose they can explain?

05 · Ownership

From adult monitoring to shared responsibility

Who sets milestones, checks evidence and decides what the next step should be?

06 · Purpose

From assigned objectives to connected work

Can the learner connect a learning goal to an interest, a practical use or a problem that matters to someone?

07 · Self-efficacy

From dependence to growing confidence

What practice, feedback or scaffold will help the learner try a harder step with appropriate support?

Framework content by Kathleen McClaskey and Katherine Tosi. The labels are prompts for reflection, not a clinical, psychometric or attainment measure.

How this connects to the programme

Make a route visible, then let learners shape it.

In a draft UnSchool project, a learner might start with a question about water use, garden soil or digital privacy. The adult and learner agree the learning goal, evidence and check-in points. The learner chooses the role and presentation format, builds an artifact and explains what changed after feedback.
