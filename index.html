1 · What ARK solves

Six common problems. What we do about each one.

Each answer is something your child does, and something you can see.

“My child finishes worksheets, but I can’t tell what they actually understand.”
What we do
  • Every lesson ends with something made: a graph, a model, a one-minute explanation.
  • The learner writes one sentence about what it shows.
  • It goes straight into the portfolio.

What you see: one dated piece of work per lesson, in your child’s own words.

“They need structure, but one-size-fits-all isn’t working.”
What we do
  • Choose one of three pathways and an age band that fits, not a grade.
  • Every lesson runs in the same order: Learn 15 min, Try 30, Build 30, Show 15.
  • A one-page plan sets up to three goals.

What you see: the plan, and a two-minute lesson log you tick off together.

“They have a big interest that school ignores.”
What we do
  • UnSchool turns the interest into a project: a question, evidence and three checkpoints.
  • The learner chooses the role and how to present it.
  • An adult agrees the goals and checks progress.

What you see: a written learning contract and a project log.

“I want screen time to mean making, not scrolling.”
What we do
  • LIBERVERSE learners write code, check AI answers and protect their privacy.
  • They design a city and practise making fair rules.
  • Every module has a print version for screen-free days.

What you see: a working game or script, a privacy checklist, an AI check log.

“I want practical life skills, not just academics.”
What we do
  • Ten skill families: repair, growing food, cooking, water, first aid, energy, navigation, emergency readiness, money and tools.
  • Each task gets harder with age, always with adult supervision.

What you see: a finished task, like a patched bike tube or a household emergency plan.

“I’m not a teacher. What do I actually have to do?”
What we do
  • Lessons, examples and answers come in the pack.
  • You set up the time and ask: “What did you make today?”
  • Every four lessons, you look at the portfolio together.

What you see: about 2 minutes a lesson, plus 10 minutes every fourth lesson.

3 · The portfolio

Five pieces of work. One clear record of learning.

Every pathway builds the same five-piece portfolio. The examples below change with the age band you picked above.

    Any format countsDrawing, writing, speaking, a model, a photo of a build, code or a chart. A camera is never required.
    An adult checks each pieceA parent, guardian or pod facilitator ticks it off. Every four lessons, you review it together.
    Private by defaultUse a nickname. The portfolio builder saves only on your device. Nothing is published.

    Badges, not money.

    A finished five-piece portfolio earns a printable certificate for that age band. Learners never get crypto, tokens or Liberland Merits. The Proof Kit can let someone check the work later without seeing your child’s name.

    Six weeks. One river. One recommendation.

    An online project using real river data. The Danube is one test river; learners can also choose the Mississippi, Amazon, Nile, Mekong or a river near home. One 90-minute online session a week, plus an optional project block at home.

    Where it runsOnline, with print packs for screen-free days.

    6 · What the format actually is

    Print at home, study online or meet in your own pod.

    One learning spine, three practical ways to use it. Choose the setting that fits your family or learning community.

    Offline-friendly

    Un-screen pack

    Print lesson cards, field journal and data sheets. An adult supports the schedule and supervises activities that need it.

    Flexible pace

    Online course

    Work through modules with low-bandwidth and private portfolio options. Cameras are not required; paper, audio and visual evidence can count.

    Local group

    Pod or microschool

    A small group uses its own home, library or other suitable venue, with a named adult facilitator responsible for the setting.

    Nothing runs at ARK Village yet.

    Questions families ask

    No pressure to enrol. Start with the details.

    What does a normal week look like?

    One or two lessons of 60–90 minutes, each ending with one piece of work filed in the portfolio. You spend about two minutes a lesson checking in, and ten minutes every fourth lesson reviewing the portfolio together.

    Can children earn Liberland Merits or crypto?

    No. Learners never get crypto wallets, tokens, payments or Liberland Merits. A finished portfolio earns a printable certificate for the learner’s age band instead.

    Does the river test run at ARK Village?

    Not yet. The ARK River Pilot runs online, and the Danube is one test river among five. ARK Village in Serbia is a possible future option only.

    Can we buy a plan today?

    No. Prices are drafts only. The site does not accept applications, payment details or enrolments.

    What is the Proof Kit?

    A working prototype for checking portfolio work. A facilitator can sign a learner’s work after parent consent, print a certificate and let anyone check it later. Files never leave the browser. Open the Proof Kit.
