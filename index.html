The point is what learners can do

A project, a portfolio and the confidence to explain the work.

The draft programme is designed so a learner does more than finish a worksheet: learn key ideas, use evidence, make or improve something, present it and reflect on what to do next. These are intended outcomes, not yet proven claims from a completed cohort.

Knowledge in use

Explain an idea with evidence

Use science, data, source-reading and civic reasoning to support a claim, name its limits and respond to questions.

Work to show

Keep a record of progress

Build a portfolio of maps, observations, data displays, drafts, prototypes, feedback and a final explanation or pitch.

Habits to practise

Plan, make, review, revise

Set a question, agree a next step, meet checkpoints and use feedback to improve the work. This is coached practice, not a promise of instant independence.

What the work could look like

  1. Question + map: an annotated place or system map and a learner-chosen question.
  2. Observations + data: a field journal, tally or simple graph, with a note on what it cannot prove.
  3. Pattern + design: a habitat/resource sketch and a small model, sign or other low-risk prototype.
  4. Revision + pitch: feedback, one recorded design change and a short evidence-backed recommendation.

This is a sample sequence derived from the draft lesson plan, not a real learner’s work or a claim that a cohort has completed it. The plan is six weekly 90-minute sessions, with an optional supervised 30–60-minute project block.

The 7 Continuums · a design lens, not the sales pitch

Learner leadership is built in stages, with adults still accountable.

Self-direction does not mean “leave the learner alone.” We use the 7 Continuums of Learner Agency as a reflection framework for moving from teacher-guided practice, through shared decisions, toward greater learner ownership as readiness grows.

The supported bridge: guided → co-designed → learner-led

A learner does not have to start at the most independent stage. In the structured middle, an adult teaches essential knowledge, co-agrees the project, names evidence and checks progress. The learner’s choices grow within those clear guardrails.

1 · Guided foundation

Adults establish core knowledge, routines and clear success criteria. Learners practise choices with examples and support.

2 · Co-designed bridge

Learner and adult agree a goal, timeline and proof. Checkpoints make it visible when the learner is stuck or needs more instruction.

3 · Learner-led work

The learner proposes a route, creates and revises the work, explains decisions and reflects on responsibility. Adults remain available and accountable.

These are design stages, not a fixed ladder, diagnosis or scored measure. Progress can vary by subject, task and moment. Open the private 7-dimension learner-agency check-in. It supports reflection, not ranking; it does not save responses.
