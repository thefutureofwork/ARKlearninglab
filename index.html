You don’t need to be a Liberland expert to start.

Liberland is one case study in the programme, not a membership test. Learners examine evidence, compare viewpoints and reach their own conclusions. Families do not need to agree with Liberland’s political ideas to use the learning materials.

One shared core · three routes

Choose the way your learner learns.

Every route builds knowledge, careful use of evidence, communication and practical capability. The route changes the pacing and project, not the core goals.

Liberland Academy

Clear lessons, one step at a time. Eight Science & Civics units with direct teaching, guided investigation and a portfolio item for each lesson.

UnSchool

Self-directed projects with structure. Learners choose a question and work with a mentor to agree the evidence, timeline and next steps.

LIBERVERSE Learning Lab

Digital skills for real life. Privacy, data, coding, AI and digital governance, with paper-first and low-bandwidth options.

Student partnership

Learners help shape the work.

Choice is more than picking a worksheet. Learners can help choose inquiry questions, project roles and ways to show what they know, then review how a unit worked.

Choose a question

Pick or adapt an inquiry question connected to the shared learning goals.

Choose how to show learning

Use writing, a model, a map, a talk, data, a practical demonstration or a portfolio.

Help improve the programme

Share feedback at checkpoints. Adults report back: “you said, we changed” or explain why a change is not possible.

Choice with clear guardrails.

Teachers and mentors still teach essential knowledge and keep common outcomes, access, safety and adult responsibilities clear. Student partnership does not mean asking children to carry legal or safeguarding responsibility.

Safety is part of the skill.

Each finished activity card should state outcomes, materials, adult notes, learner choices and evidence. First aid, food safety, fire and higher-risk tools require appropriate qualified instruction and supervision. No folk cures or unsafe shortcuts.

A case study, not a belief test

Why put Liberland in a civics curriculum?

It gives learners a live, contested case for questions they meet in many communities: how are rules made, who decides, what counts as evidence, and how should people cooperate?

First, what is a microstate? It’s an informal label for a very small state; there is no single agreed size cutoff. Recognized small states such as Monaco and San Marino can be compared with other political arrangements. Liberland is different: its claim to statehood and the land is disputed, and it is not recognized by any UN member state. We teach that distinction plainly, not as settled statehood.

In class, learners compare sources and viewpoints. They can study personal liberty, voluntary cooperation, markets and digital governance alongside other political traditions, public services and shared-resource models. Students are not expected to agree with Liberland, a teacher, or one another; claims should be tested against evidence and reasoned arguments.

Sources: Columbia Journal of International Affairs on microstate definitions; Liberland’s account; Liberland’s current constitutional page; Croatian Ministry of Foreign Affairs statement. Liberland’s Constitution page says its Constitution is being drafted.
