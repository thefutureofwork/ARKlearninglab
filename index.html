1 · The point is what learners can do

A project, a portfolio and the confidence to explain the work.

The draft programme is designed so a learner does more than finish a worksheet: learn key ideas, use evidence, make or improve something, present it and reflect on what to do next. These are intended outcomes, not yet proven claims from a completed cohort.

Knowledge in use

Explain an idea with evidence

Use science, data, source-reading and civic reasoning to support a claim, name its limits and respond to questions.

Work to show

Keep a record of progress

Build a portfolio of maps, observations, data displays, drafts, prototypes, feedback and a final explanation or pitch.

Habits to practise

Plan, make, review, revise

Set a question, agree a next step, meet checkpoints and use feedback to improve the work. This is coached practice, not a promise of instant independence.

Illustrative six-week portfolio · ARK River Pilot draft

From river question to recommendation

  1. Question + map: an annotated place or system map and a learner-chosen question.
  2. Observations + data: a field journal, tally or simple graph, with a note on what it cannot prove.
  3. Pattern + design: a habitat/resource sketch and a small model, sign or other low-risk prototype.
  4. Revision + pitch: feedback, one recorded design change and a short evidence-backed recommendation.

This is an illustrative sequence derived from the draft lesson plan, not real learner work or a claim that a cohort has completed it.

3 · ARK River Pilot · six-week draft

How can we help ARK care for its water, living things and resources?

A place-based learning plan at ARK Village on the Danube. One 90-minute session a week, plus an optional supervised project block. Learners choose the question; adults keep it safe.

  1. Read the placeAnnotated map and one learner question.
  2. WaterObserve, measure and name what evidence cannot prove.
  3. Who lives here?Habitat sketch, survey and species tally.
  4. Food & resourcesTrace a resource journey and look for a useful improvement.
  5. DesignBuild and test a small, low-risk prototype.
  6. Share & decidePresent an evidence-backed recommendation and reflect.
Illustrative plan, not a live cohort.

5 · What the format actually is

Print at home, study online or meet in your own pod.

6 · Choice with guardrails

Self-direction does not mean “leave the learner alone.”

Learner leadership grows in stages. Adults teach, check in and remain accountable while the learner’s choices expand.

Guided foundation

Adults establish core knowledge, routines and clear success criteria. Learners practise choices with examples and support.

Co-designed bridge

Learner and adult agree a goal, timeline and proof. Checkpoints make it visible when the learner needs help or more instruction.

Learner-led work

The learner proposes a route, creates and revises the work, explains decisions and reflects on responsibility. Adults stay available and accountable.

These are design stages, not a ranking or diagnosis. The 7 Continuums of Learner Agency are a reflection framework; open the private check-in.

7 · Support, readiness and draft pricing

The practical details, without the guesswork.

Weekly time

Depends on the route

Core Academy: 60–90 minutes per lesson. UnSchool: roughly 2–4 project hours a week. LIBERVERSE: about 90 minutes a week in its draft year model.

Mentor support

Still being defined

Draft plans describe periodic kickoff, review or facilitator check-ins. The mentor roster and mentor-to-learner ratio are not yet confirmed.
