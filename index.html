First, the plain version

What is ARK Learning Lab?

A flexible learning programme for families, learning pods and schools who want more than worksheets: clear teaching, age-aware learning goals and projects connected to real questions.

What learners study

Knowledge for a changing world

Science, civics, literacy, economics, practical capability and digital life. The materials connect concepts to a learner’s home, community, a fictional place or the Danube region.

How they learn

Learn it, try it, make something

Short direct lessons build the core knowledge. Learners investigate, make or explain something, keep evidence of their work and reflect on what to try next.

Designed to complement schooling, not replace it.

ARK Learning Lab is a non-formal enrichment programme. It does not award school credit or qualifications, and it does not replace families’ local education requirements.

One learner · one primary pathway

Three ways into the learning.

Each pathway has a different structure and emphasis. All are designed around shared outcomes: build knowledge, use evidence honestly, communicate clearly and apply learning in a project.

Structured learning

Core Academy

Clear, sequenced teaching across the core subjects, with guided practice and age-appropriate ways to show learning. The proposed Science & Civics Pack is its Liberland-focused case-study strand.

How it works

A simple learning cycle.

01 · Choose

Pick a pathway and question

Start with the learner’s age, interests and preferred structure. Agree what they are working toward.

02 · Investigate

Learn, practise and build

Use lessons, readings or prepared materials, then apply them to a project at home, in a pod or online.

03 · Show and reflect

Keep evidence of learning

Use writing, a model, a map, data, a demonstration or a conversation. Review progress and choose a next step.

Choose a setting

Same learning goals. Flexible delivery.

Print first

At home

Use downloadable or printed lesson materials. A parent or guardian supports the schedule and supervises activities that need an adult.

Flexible pace

Online

Work through low-bandwidth modules. Cameras are not required, and paper, audio and visual evidence can count.

Local community

In a pod

A small group meets at its own home, library or other suitable venue, with an adult facilitator responsible for the setting.

A concrete example

A six-week river inquiry.

The draft ARK River Pilot asks: How can we help ARK care for its water, living things and resources? Learners help choose the focus, then build a small piece of evidence each week.

Weeks 1–2

Notice and measure

Map the place, ask a testable question and observe water safely. The evidence might be an annotated map, a simple data display or a note about what the data cannot prove.

Weeks 3–4

Look for patterns

Explore habitats, food and resource flows. Learners use a tally, sketch, story map or prepared dataset rather than disturbing wildlife or handling unsafe materials.

Weeks 5–6

Design and share

Make a small prototype or proposal, ask for feedback, explain the evidence and reflect on what could be improved.

An optional case study, not a belief test

Why does Liberland appear here?

Liberland gives learners a source-based way to examine questions that matter in many communities: how rules are made, who decides, what evidence supports a claim and how people cooperate.

Compare, don’t prescribe

Learners distinguish established facts, competing claims, proposals and opinions. They can examine Liberland’s ideas alongside other political traditions and draw their own conclusions.

Be precise about constitutional status

Liberland’s official page says its Constitution is still being drafted and reproduces the Articles of the Provisional Government. Section 1 says those Articles remain supreme law until a Constitution enters into force. That is civic source material, not a claim that this programme is a government school.

Does my learner need to support Liberland?

No. Liberland is one optional, contested civics case study. Learners are expected to examine sources and viewpoints, not adopt a political position.

Is this a school or a qualification?

No. ARK Learning Lab is a draft enrichment programme, not an accredited school or qualification. Families remain responsible for local education requirements.

Can we book a visit to ARK Village?

Not currently. The site-based option is not open for booking and will require confirmed local legal, safeguarding, staffing and site arrangements.

Can I buy a package today?

No. Enrolment and payments are not open. Prices are drafts only; no payment details should be sent through this website.
