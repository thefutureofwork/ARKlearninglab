Part 1 · What Liberland is

A country project on the Danube.

2015Declared on 13 April 2015 by Vít Jedlička and Jana Markovičová.
≈ 7 km²About the size of a small town, on the west bank of the Danube.
≈ 1,000Citizens, by Liberland's own count, living around the world.
0UN member states that recognise it as a country.
  1. Where it is.Between Croatia and Serbia, at about 45°46′N 18°52′E, in an area called Gornja Siga. The nearest towns are Zmajevac (Croatia) and Bački Monoštor (Serbia).
  2. Why that land, and who disagrees.Croatia and Serbia have not yet agreed their border along the river. Liberland says this pocket of land is claimed by neither country and was terra nullius (no man's land), so its founders declared a state there. Croatia's Ministry of Foreign Affairs says the area is a delimitation zone between Croatia and Serbia, under Croatian border police control, that it is not terra nullius, and that visitors must follow Croatian and EU law. Learners study both positions.
  3. What it says it stands for.Its motto is “To Live and Let Live”. It emphasises personal freedom, voluntary cooperation, free markets, low taxes and digital government (including its own blockchain). These are Liberland's own positions; learners compare them with other approaches.
  4. How it is run today.Liberland describes a provisional government chaired by its president. Its Constitution is still being drafted; the Articles of the Provisional Government apply in the meantime.
  5. What it cannot do yet.The Croatian police restrict access to the land. Liberland says small groups, mostly campers and people living on boats, have stayed there since 2023. It has signed memoranda with some parties abroad, but formal recognition by UN member states is still missing.

Sources: liberland.org/about, liberland.org/constitution and the Croatian Ministry of Foreign Affairs statement “On Virtual Narratives at Croatia’s Borders” (June 2015). Liberland's figures are its own and may change.

Part 2 · Why this programme exists

Big questions, small case study.

How are rules made? Who decides? What counts as evidence? A small, contested project makes those everyday civic questions easy to see.

1. A real case study

Most learners only meet rights, rules and markets in a textbook. Liberland is a live example of people trying to design them. Learners study real documents, real debates and real trade-offs, and compare them with established countries.

2. Thinking, not believing

We teach learners to sort fact, claim, proposal and opinion. They study Liberland's ideas critically, compare them with other political traditions and draw their own conclusions. Agreeing with Liberland is never a learning goal.

3. Choice with responsibility

Learners make real choices about questions, projects and how they show their learning, and take responsibility for the result. Adults keep safety and core goals clear.

4. Digital life done well

Our LIBERVERSE Learning Lab teaches privacy, data, code and AI so learners can use technology safely and build with it. Paper-first options are always available.

5. Learning that travels

There is no school building to commute to. The programme works at home, online and in small local pods, wherever your family lives.

6. The river as a classroom

The Danube links the science lessons (water, habitats, energy, food) to the civics lessons (maps, borders, claims). The river pilot runs online, with the Danube as one of five test rivers. ARK Village in Apatin, Serbia, is on the same river but is not open yet, and nothing runs there.

Our mission

Help learners aged 6 to 18 become curious, careful thinkers who can weigh evidence, make fair rules, trade honestly and build useful things, at home, online or together in pods.

Part 3 · What this programme is not

Clear limits.

Not a state school

ARK Learning Lab is not an official institution of the Liberland government and does not speak for it.

Not accredited

It is an enrichment programme. It does not award recognised diplomas. Families follow their own local education rules.

Not on Liberland's land

No lesson takes place on the territory itself. Lessons happen at home, online and in pods. Nothing runs at ARK Village in Serbia yet.

Where

Where do the lessons happen?

Most learning happens where the learner already lives. You choose the place. Every seat includes both the print pack and online access, so you can mix them.

Place 1 · most common

At home

The learner works at your kitchen table or desk from the printed pack. A parent/guardian sets up the time and checks the work.

Place 2

Online, anywhere

The same lessons as short online modules on a computer or tablet, at home or travelling. Mostly self-paced; the River Pilot adds one 90-minute online session a week.

Place 3 · pods only

Your pod's own venue

A mini Pod meets in the lead parent/guardian's home. A full Pod meets in a room its facilitator arranges (a home, library or hall). We send the materials; we do not provide the room.

Not open yet

ARK Village, Apatin, Serbia

A possible future option only. Nothing runs at ARK Village yet: no lessons, no visit days and no river test, and nothing can be booked. Any future activity there would need safeguarding sign-off and legal review first.

Mentoring meetings are always online (video, or phone if you prefer). No lessons take place on Liberland's own land: access to that land is restricted by the Croatian police, so the programme is built to work at home and online.
