People first · safety before activity

Safeguarding is a launch condition.

Children and young people must be able to learn, raise a concern and decline an activity without pressure. Adults remain accountable for supervision, safety and lawful operation.

Draft framework · not yet an approved or operational policyThis page describes proposed safeguards and the work required before any programme involving minors begins. A named safeguarding lead, alternate contact, local legal review and operational procedures must be confirmed and shared with families before enrolment. Do not treat this draft as proof that a programme is currently ready to receive children.

Proposed operating commitments

Safeguarding is designed into the programme.

Clear adult accountability

Name a safeguarding lead and an alternate reviewer before participation. Learners and families need a private, understandable way to raise concerns.

Safe, visible relationships

Adopt a staff code of conduct, professional boundaries and rules for one-to-one contact, communications, transport and supervision, based on local advice and the activity risk assessment.

Consent with real choice

Explain activities, risks, supervision and data use in advance. Participation consent, optional photography and public sharing of work are separate choices. Offer an equivalent safe activity when a learner opts out.

Privacy by default

Collect only information needed for participation and safety. Limit access, set retention and deletion rules, and never publish identifiable learner details, portfolios, learning plans or incident records without appropriate authorization.

Accessible participation

Ask what adjustments help a learner take part. Offer indoor, low-resource and non-digital alternatives where feasible, without making access depend on mobility, language fluency or a camera.

Pause when safety changes

Any responsible adult can stop an activity if conditions become unsafe. Site access, weather, water, wildlife, tools and materials all need boundaries and adult oversight.

What is required versus what is proposed?

These are programme design commitments, not a statement of specific Serbian legal duties. Local counsel and the responsible operator must identify applicable requirements, approve procedures and confirm who is accountable before launch.

Before, during and after

One consistent safety routine across delivery modes.

Un-screen pack

Give families the activity notes, age guidance, materials and adult-supervision expectations. Outdoor activities and tools require an adult; provide a safe indoor or prepared-data alternative.

Online course

Use private course spaces and age-appropriate moderation. No public learner profiles or required camera use. Define permitted contact channels, response coverage and how concerns reach an adult.

ARK on-site

Before booking, approve cohort capacity, adult coverage, check-in/out, approved routes, water and tool boundaries, first aid, weather alternatives, emergency contacts and site permissions.

Learning activities

No tasting unknown water, unsupervised sampling, handling or disturbing wildlife, raw waste, unapproved chemicals or unsupervised higher-risk tools. Qualified instruction is needed for first aid and other higher-risk activities.

  1. Prepare: confirm the activity, site permissions, hazards, access needs, supervision plan and emergency arrangements.
  2. Orient: tell learners and families what will happen, who is responsible, what choices they have and how to ask for help.
  3. Supervise: keep to approved boundaries, check attendance and conditions, and stop or adapt activities when needed.
  4. Close the loop: record incidents through the approved private process, communicate appropriately with families and review what should change.

Specific staffing ratios, suitability checks, reporting duties, records and retention periods must be determined under applicable law and qualified local advice; this page does not set legal standards.

Listen · protect · review

Concerns should be taken seriously and handled privately.

If a learner is in immediate danger, adults should act to secure immediate safety and contact local emergency services as appropriate. For other concerns, the proposed process is:

  1. Listen calmly. Take the learner seriously; do not promise secrecy or investigate beyond what is needed to understand immediate safety.
  2. Protect first. Pause an activity or separate a learner from an immediate risk. Get help from the designated safeguarding lead or alternate.
  3. Record minimally. Use the approved private record process, capturing only necessary facts and actions. Do not circulate details in group chats or public materials.
  4. Escalate and communicate. Follow the reviewed local procedure for guardian notification and any required external referral. If a concern is about the safeguarding lead, use the alternate route.
  5. Review fairly. A person independent of the concern should review non-urgent decisions where possible. Protective steps should not wait for an appeal.
There is no named reporting contact on this draft page.

A named lead, alternate, contact method and expected response window must be supplied in family materials before enrolment. This page is not an incident-reporting channel.

Required before a minors' programme opens

Close these gates before the first cohort.

  • Accountable peopleName a safeguarding lead, deputy and independent escalation route; confirm coverage for every session.
  • Local legal reviewConfirm requirements for education status, child protection, consent, privacy, staffing, site use and reporting in Serbia.
  • Staff readinessApprove role descriptions, conduct rules, locally appropriate suitability checks, training and supervision arrangements.
  • Activity risk plansAssess each site and activity; document routes, water boundaries, tools, weather, first aid and alternatives.
  • Family informationProvide schedule, supervision, consent, emergency contacts, access arrangements, privacy notice and complaint route before booking.
  • Private information handlingDefine who can access records, why they are collected, retention and secure deletion before collecting data.
  • Digital and third-party reviewReview platform age terms, moderation, data flows, contact permissions and public-sharing settings.
  • Incident practiceAgree how adults pause activities, support learners, document and escalate concerns, then rehearse the process.

Liberland Academy is currently presented as an enrichment programme, not an accredited school or qualification. Any on-site programme at ARK Village, Apatin, should proceed only after the responsible operator confirms local legal and safeguarding readiness.

Private ARK informs the Academy's configurable-menu and itinerary design. It is a hospitality/group-programme pattern, not a child-protection, school-compliance or privacy standard. This safeguarding framework is a proposed extension, not an approved policy.
