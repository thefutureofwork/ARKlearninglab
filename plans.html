The rule of the plan

Start narrow. Measure honestly. Adjust without guilt.

1–3 goalsStart with one. Add a second only once the first runs on its own.
A baseline firstA quick check or recent work sample before any goal is written.
Named ownersEvery goal says who tracks it and when it is reviewed.
FIELD 1

Learner profile

Strengths, interests, preferred ways of learning and showing work, access needs.

FIELD 2

Baseline

Current level from a diagnostic, knowledge check or portfolio item, with the date collected.

FIELD 3

SMART goals

One to three: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and due by a named lesson or date.

FIELD 4

Strategies and supports

The method, tool, scaffold or mentor time that will get there, and who provides it.

FIELD 5

Monitoring plan

What is checked, by whom, how often, and where it is recorded.

FIELD 6

Agreement

Learner, parent or guardian and teacher or mentor confirm the plan and the first review.

Where it fits.

The plan sits on top of the shared curriculum, never instead of it. Goals personalize pace, support and evidence format; core outcomes for each age band stay the same. In UnSchool, the plan is the learning contract. Stewardship can be a goal too: a care routine kept up, a resource tracked, a promise to a team honoured.

Build it in about 45 minutes

Five steps, in order.

  1. Gather evidence and write the profile. Use a short diagnostic, the last unit's knowledge check or portfolio work, plus what the learner says is hard and what they enjoy.
  2. Agree one to three SMART goals with the learner. The learner helps write them. Goals come from what actually matters this term, not a wish list.
  3. Choose strategies and name owners. Extra practice, a scaffold, mentor time, an audio story, a different evidence format: each with the adult responsible.
  4. Set the review rhythm. A micro-check every lesson, a progress note at the end of every unit, a full review at the end of the sequence.
  5. Share it and hold the first check-in within two lessons. A plan in a drawer helps nobody.
Academic goal · Unit 1, ages 9–11By Lesson 4 of The Danube as a Living System, Mila will raise her vocabulary check from 3 of 8 to 7 of 8 key terms, tracked by a two-minute recall check at the end of each lesson and logged in her field journal.
Self-management goal · UnSchool, ages 12–14Over the next 8 lessons, Teo will bring a complete evidence item to 7 of 8 sessions, tracked on a lesson checklist his mentor initials, with a choice of written, drawn or spoken evidence.

Names are fictional examples. Both goals carry a number, a deadline and a tracking method inside the sentence.

Review rhythm · counted in lessons, not weeks

Check small, often.

Every lesson

Micro-check

Two minutes: did the agreed task happen? One tally or tick in the log. The learner answers one prompt: what helped, what got in the way.

Every 4 lessons · end of unit

Progress note

Compare the evidence to baseline. A 15-minute check-in: What's working? What's stuck? What changes next?

After 32 lessons · or term end

Full review

Reset goals, update the learner profile, celebrate what was met and decide what carries forward.

Adjustment rules.

Goal met at two check-ins in a row with little effort: raise the bar. Goal missed three check-ins running: change the goal or the strategy, not just the pressure on the learner. Date every change with a one-line reason.

Learner

Does the agreed task and answers one reflection prompt per check-in. Helps write and revise goals.

Parent or guardian

Protects the learning time, sets up a calm workspace, encourages without nagging, joins unit check-ins.

Teacher or mentor

Teaches, collects the evidence, proposes adjustments when the data calls for it, keeps the plan record.

Expect real gains over time. In a multi-year RAND study of personalized-learning schools, the stronger maths gains tended to appear in the second year. Confidence and consistency often improve before scores do.

Boundaries

A planning tool, not a legal plan.

A personalized learning plan is voluntary and flexible. It is not an Individualized Education Program, a diagnosis or a statement of legal entitlements; learners with additional needs should be supported through the appropriate formal process where they live. Plans are stored privately, shared only with the people named in them, and never published or placed on a blockchain. Any learner data handling follows the draft safeguarding framework, which must be finalized before enrolment.
