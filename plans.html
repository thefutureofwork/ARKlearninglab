Personalized learning plans · a plain-language guide

Who does what, where, and when?

A learning plan turns one learner's starting point into a small, visible next step. Here is exactly who it is for, what goes in it, where it is used, when it is reviewed, why it helps, and how to make one.

Start here

The whole idea, in six answers.

A plan is a working agreement between a learner and the adults supporting them. It personalizes the route and support, not the shared learning outcomes.

Who?

Learner + supporting adults

For learners ages 6–18 in Liberland Academy, UnSchool, or LIBERVERSE Learning Lab. The learner helps choose and review the goal. A parent or guardian protects time and shares relevant context. A teacher or mentor teaches, tracks evidence, and coordinates the support.

What?

One short, usable plan

A private, printable page with the learner's strengths, a dated starting point, one to three measurable goals, agreed supports, a named person to check progress, and the next review date. Start with one goal, not a wish list.

Where?

Where learning happens

Use it at home with the Un-screen pack, alongside online lessons, or during a supervised ARK on-site programme. It sits beside the shared curriculum; it does not replace lessons. Keep the completed plan in the family's or provider's private records.

When?

Before the sequence, then often

Set it before or during the first lessons, using a recent work sample or quick baseline check. Hold the first check-in within two lessons, do a two-minute check each lesson, review every four lessons, and reset after 32 lessons or at term end.

Why?

Make support specific

So the learner, family, and mentor agree on what to practise and can spot early if a strategy is helping. Use it to build on strengths, make pace or evidence format more accessible, and keep the learner moving toward the same age-band outcomes.

How?

Agree, try, check, adjust

Set aside about 45 minutes. Look at evidence, agree one goal with the learner, choose a support, name who checks it, and set the next date. If the goal is missed three checks in a row, change the goal or support, not just the pressure.

What a plan does not change.

It can personalize pace, scaffolding, support, and evidence format. It does not remove core learning outcomes, promise a particular result, or replace teaching. In UnSchool it can form part of the learner's learning contract. Stewardship goals are welcome too, such as maintaining a care routine or following through on a team commitment.

A 45-minute planning conversation

Six steps, with the learner in the room.

Bring one recent piece of work, the learner's own view of what feels easy or hard, and the adult who will follow through. A plan only helps when it is used in the next lesson.

  1. Start with strengths. Ask what the learner enjoys, what they are proud of, and how they like to learn or show understanding.
  2. Choose one baseline. Use a short check or real work sample. Write down the result and date; do not guess from memory.
  3. Write one SMART goal together. Name the skill, the measure, the target, and the lesson or date it is due. Keep it realistic.
  4. Pick the support. Choose a practice routine, scaffold, mentor check-in, or evidence format that directly helps with this goal.
  5. Name the owner and evidence. Say who teaches or provides the support, who checks progress, and where the evidence is kept.
  6. Book the first check-in. Put it within two lessons. Decide now what to keep, change, or stop if the approach is not helping.

Mila and Teo are fictional examples. A strong goal says what will change, how much, by when, and who will check.

When the plan is checked

Short feedback loops beat end-of-term surprises.

Every lesson · 2 minutes

Micro-check

Did the agreed task happen? Log one tally or piece of evidence. Ask the learner what helped and what got in the way.

Every 4 lessons · about 15 minutes

Progress conversation

Compare new evidence to the baseline. Keep what works; name what is stuck; agree one change if needed.

After 32 lessons · or term end

Full review

Celebrate progress, update the profile, decide what carries forward, and set the next sequence's goal.

Simple adjustment rule.

If a goal is met at two check-ins in a row with little effort, raise the challenge. If it is missed at three check-ins in a row, revisit the goal or the support. Date the change and write one reason. Missing a target is information, not a penalty.

Learner

Helps choose the goal, tries the agreed task, and says what is or is not working.

Parent or guardian

Helps protect learning time, encourages without nagging, and joins agreed check-ins.

Teacher or mentor

Teaches the skill, gathers evidence, provides the agreed support, and proposes changes based on progress.

Privacy and boundaries

Keep the learner's information theirs.

Use only information needed to support learning. This page's builder runs in your browser and has no save, send, or checkout function; the site still loads its fonts from Google Fonts. Do not put sensitive details in the plan. Share a completed copy only with people the learner and guardian agree should see it, and store it according to the finalized safeguarding and privacy process.
