Personalized learning plans

One page. One goal. Checked every lesson.

A learning plan says where a learner is now, the next thing they're working on, what will help, and who checks. It takes about 45 minutes to write, and the builder on this page is free.

Start here

The plan in six questions.

Who

The learner, plus two adults

  • Any learner aged 6–18 in Liberland Academy, UnSchool or LIBERVERSE Learning Lab.
  • The learner helps write it. A parent or guardian and a teacher or mentor agree to it.
What

A single printable page

  • Strengths, a dated starting point and 1 to 3 measurable goals.
  • Each goal names its support and who checks it.
Where

Wherever the lessons happen

  • At home with the print pack, online, or at ARK on-site sessions.
  • It goes with the curriculum and doesn't replace any lessons.
When

At the start, then often

  • Write it at the start of a sequence, with a quick baseline check.
  • First check within 2 lessons, a quick check each lesson, a review every 4, a reset after 32.
Why

So help is specific

  • Everyone knows what to practise and can see early if a support isn't working.
  • Pace and support change. The shared learning outcomes stay the same.
How

Agree, try, check, adjust

  • Look at real work, agree one goal, pick a support and a checker, and set a date.
  • Goal missed 3 times in a row? Change the goal or the support. Don't just push harder.

What a plan can't change

It can adjust pace, support and the format of evidence. It can't drop the core outcomes, and it doesn't guarantee a result. In UnSchool, the plan forms part of the learning contract.

How to make one · about 45 minutes

Six steps, with the learner in the room.

Bring one recent piece of work, the learner's view of what feels easy or hard, and the adult who will follow through.

  1. Start with strengths. Ask what they enjoy, what they're proud of and how they like to learn.
  2. Take one baseline. Use a short check or a real piece of work. Write down the result and the date.
  3. Write one goal together. Name the skill, the measure, the target and the lesson it's due by.
  4. Pick one support. A practice routine, scaffold, mentor check-in or evidence format that helps with this goal.
  5. Name who checks. Say who provides the support, who checks progress and where the evidence is kept.
  6. Book the first check-in. Within two lessons. A plan in a drawer helps nobody.

Self-management · UnSchool · ages 12–14Over the next 8 lessons, Teo will bring a finished evidence item to 7 of 8 sessions. His mentor initials a checklist. Teo picks written, drawn or spoken evidence.

Mila and Teo are made-up examples. A strong goal says what changes, how much, by when, and who checks.

When it's checked

Small checks, often.

Every lesson · 2 min

Quick check

Did the agreed task happen? Make one tally. Ask what helped and what got in the way.

Every 4 lessons · 15 min

Progress chat

Compare the evidence with the baseline. Keep what works and change one thing if needed.

After 32 lessons

Full review

Celebrate what was met, update the profile and set the next goal.

The adjustment rule

Goal met twice in a row with ease? Raise it. Missed three times in a row? Change the goal or the support. Date each change and give one reason.

Learner

Helps choose the goal, does the agreed task and says what's working.

Parent or guardian

Protects learning time, encourages without nagging and joins check-ins.

Teacher or mentor

Teaches, collects evidence, provides the support and suggests changes.

Common questions

Straight answers.

Is this an IEP or a legal plan?

No. It's a voluntary planning tool. It isn't a diagnosis, a statement of legal rights or a replacement for formal support where you live. If a learner needs formal provision, go through the official process locally.

Do I have to buy anything?

No. The builder and the steps on this page are free. The paid options add a mentor and printed materials, and they're still drafts.

Where does my information go?

Nowhere. The builder runs in your browser and doesn't save or send anything. The page only loads its fonts from Google Fonts. Keep printed plans private and share them only with the people named on them.

What if the goal is wrong?

Change it. After three missed checks in a row, adjust the goal or the support, write the date and one reason, then carry on.

Will it guarantee better results?

No. Results in personalized-learning research vary from school to school. A plan helps everyone stay clear and consistent, but it can't promise any particular result.
