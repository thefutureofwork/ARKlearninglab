For parents · learning plans

Help your child learn one thing better. Step by step.

This page shows you exactly what to do, from start to finish. You don't need to be a teacher, and you don't need any special words. Just follow the steps in order.

Start at Step 1See all the steps
Free · takes about 45 minutes to set up

First, what is this?

A learning plan is one sheet of paper.

It says one thing your child is working on, what will help, and how you'll know it's working. That's it. You keep it on the fridge or in a folder, and you look at it for 2 minutes after each lesson.

It IS

A simple plan you make with your child. You can change it any time.

It is NOT

A test, a grade or a report card. It's not a legal or medical document either.

It works with

Liberland Academy, UnSchool and LIBERVERSE Learning Lab, at home, online or at ARK.

Words you'll see

Five words. That's all you need.

WordWhat it meansExample
Starting pointWhat your child can do today."Knows 3 of 8 river words."
GoalWhat you want them to do, and by when."Know 7 of 8 by lesson 4."
HelpThe one thing you'll do to get there."Practise the words for 2 minutes before each lesson."
CheckA quick look after a lesson to see how it went."Ask the 8 words. Count how many she knows."
MentorThe teacher or coach who helps you, if you have one."Ms. Novak, our UnSchool mentor."

The whole thing at a glance

Three parts. Ten small steps.

  1. Part A · Get readySteps 1–2 · 10 minutes
  2. Part B · Make the planSteps 3–7 · 35 minutes
  3. Part C · Use it every lessonSteps 8–10 · 2 minutes each time

Part A · Get ready

Before you sit down.

  1. 1

    Gather four things

    5 minutes

    • A pencil and this page (or the printed plan)
    • One recent piece of your child's work: a worksheet, drawing, journal page or project
    • A quiet table and 45 minutes with no phones
    • Your child. They must be part of this.
  2. 2

    Tell your child what's happening

    5 minutes

    Say something like:

    "We're going to pick one thing to get better at together. You get to help choose. It's not a test, and nobody's in trouble."

    If your child is nervous, start by talking about something they're good at.

Part B · Make the plan

Five steps, one at a time.

We'll follow a made-up example the whole way: Mila, age 9, doing the river unit, The Danube as a Living System.

  1. 3

    Write down what your child is good at

    5 minutes

    Ask: "What do you like doing? What are you proud of? How do you like to show what you know: drawing, talking, writing or building?"

    Mila's answer: "I like drawing maps. I'm good at explaining things out loud."

    Write it in your child's words. Two lines is enough.

  2. 4

    Find the starting point

    10 minutes

    Do: Pick one skill from the current unit. Do a quick check, then count the result.

    Mila: Her parent reads out 8 river words from Lesson 1. Mila explains 3 of them correctly. Starting point: 3 of 8. Write today's date next to it.

    Don't guess. If you can't count it, pick something else you can count, like words, sums, finished tasks or days on time.

  3. 5

    Pick ONE goal together

    10 minutes

    Fill in this sentence:

    By lesson ___, [child] will go from ___ to ___ of ___ [thing you counted].

    Mila: "By lesson 4, Mila will go from 3 to 7 of 8 river words."

    Make it a little hard, but possible. Only one goal. You can add more later.

  4. 6

    Choose ONE thing that will help

    5 minutes

    Ask your child: "What would help you remember?" Then pick one idea from this list or your own:

    • 2 minutes of practice before each lesson
    • Draw a picture or map for each new idea
    • Explain it out loud to you or a sibling
    • Listen to the unit's audio story first
    • A tick-box chart on the fridge

    Mila picked: "Draw a little map for each river word, and practise for 2 minutes before the lesson."

  5. 7

    Decide who checks, then sign it

    5 minutes

    Write down: who will do the check (you, a teacher or a mentor), and where you'll keep the results (a notebook, the field journal or the printed plan).

    Then: everyone signs and dates the bottom. Put it somewhere you'll see it.

    Mila: "Mum checks. Results go in Mila's field journal. Plan goes on the fridge."

Part C · Use it every lesson

The plan only works if you look at it.

  1. 8

    After every lesson: the 2-minute check

    2 minutes

    1. Do the same quick check as Step 4. (Mila: ask the 8 words again.)
    2. Write the number and the date.
    3. Ask one question: "What helped today?"

    Keep it light. Say "thanks for trying", not "you got it wrong".

  2. 9

    Every 4 lessons: a 15-minute chat

    15 minutes

    Look at the numbers together and ask three questions:

    1. "What's going well?"
    2. "What's still hard?"
    3. "Should we change anything?"

    Then use the rule below.

  3. 10

    Change the plan when the numbers tell you to

    5 minutes

    If this happens…Do this
    Goal reached easily, two checks in a rowMake the goal a bit harder.
    Getting closer slowlyKeep going. Don't change anything.
    No progress, three checks in a rowChange the help, or make the goal smaller. Don't just push harder.
    Finished all 32 lessonsCelebrate, then go back to Step 3 and pick a new goal.

    Write the date and one reason each time you change something.

When things go wrong

Stuck? Find your problem here.

My child won't do it, or gets upset.

Stop for today. Next time, let them pick the goal and the help. Make the goal smaller. Start each check with something they're good at.

I forgot to do the checks.

That's normal. Just do one at the next lesson. Stick the plan on the fridge or set a phone reminder for lesson days.

We missed some lessons.

Don't start over. Move the "by lesson" number later and carry on.

I don't know what to count.

Pick something with a number: words known, problems right, tasks finished, minutes of reading or days on time. If you have a mentor, ask them.

The goal was too hard or too easy.

Change it. That's allowed. Use the table in Step 10, write the date and keep going.

My child has extra learning needs.

This plan can help day to day, but it isn't an IEP or a formal support plan. Talk to your local school or health service about formal help.

Three common mistakes

Too many goals

Start with one. One goal you actually check beats five you forget.

Guessing

Always count. "Better at reading" can't be checked. "Reads 2 pages without help" can.

Doing it for them

Your child helps choose and does the work. Your job is to make time, cheer them on and check.

Questions

Simple answers.

Do I have to pay?

No. Everything on this page is free. Paying only adds a mentor and printed materials.

Is my information saved?

No. The plan maker works only on your device and doesn't save or send anything. Keep your printed plan private.

Is this an official school plan?

No. It's a home learning tool, not an IEP, diagnosis or legal document.

Will it guarantee better grades?

No. Nothing can promise that. It does help you and your child stay focused and notice early when something isn't working.
